# EIGHT

Game development project for YTU, built with Godot. Most programming will be AI-assisted; game direction and asset creation remain human-led.

## Open the project

1. Install **Godot 4.7.2 Standard** (not .NET) and [Git LFS](https://git-lfs.com/).
2. Clone the repository:

   ```sh
   git lfs install
   git clone https://github.com/slyim/EIGHT.git
   cd EIGHT
   git lfs pull
   ```

3. In Godot's Project Manager, import `eight/project.godot` and open it.

The existing project uses Forward Plus, Direct3D 12 on Windows, and Jolt Physics. Keep these settings unless a deliberate engine/platform change requires otherwise. The original Steam editor reports `4.7.2.stable.steam.ed1daf0bf`.

## The pentagram (main scene)

Press **F5 / Run Project** to open the pentagram hub (`eight/scenes/hub.tscn`): the seven sins sit evenly on a glowing outer ring around a pentagram, with Satan at the center. Click a sin or press **1-7** to enter its realm on that node's chosen difficulty tier. Four tiers exist (`Run.TIERS`, PLACEHOLDER numbers): **Penance**, **Purgatory**, **Damnation**, and **Abyss**. Each sin starts with Penance open, and beating a sin on a tier opens the next tier for that sin (winning an easier tier never lowers its best). Nodes show their chosen tier and `BEST <TIER>` once beaten; `<` `>` arrows flank each node to step its tier and stay hidden while only one tier is open (or while Satan is locked); from the keyboard, **Shift+1-7** (or **Shift+S** for Satan) cycles a node's tier. Unpicked nodes default to their hardest open tier. Winning a realm seals that sin: its relic glows on its pedestal with a gold chord to the center, and the hub plays a sealing ripple the first time. With all seven relics in place Satan unlocks and offers up to the lowest tier beaten across all seven sins; click the center or press **S** to summon him. Progress (sealed sins, whether Satan has fallen, and the `[best]` tier beaten per map in save version 2) is saved to `user://progress.cfg` the moment a realm is won; a tier counts only for a map the save records as won, any won map with a missing tier (every version-1 save) or a damaged one counts as Penance, and other missing or damaged values are ignored or start fresh. The hub is crisp vector UI drawn from the shared theme; its pentagram, pedestals and relics are code-drawn shapes.

Each sin has its own map scene in `eight/scenes/sins/` (`pride.tscn` ... `sloth.tscn`, and `satan.tscn`). Each inherits `eight/scenes/run.tscn` and sets its `sin_index`; its obstacles and Satan's shrines are real `Breakable` nodes under `Obstacles` that you can move, add, or delete in the editor (a sin map left with no shrines summons its sin right after landing), and its lava pools are the `lava_layout` array on the root (one entry per pool, each a list of `[center, radius]` blobs). The layouts are PLACEHOLDER, generated once by `godot --headless --path eight --script res://tools/bake_sin_maps.gd`, which never overwrites an existing map scene (delete one to regenerate it). Decoration is still scattered from the map's seed. `eight/scenes/game.tscn` hosts the chosen map in the pixel-perfect view described under [Pixel-perfect rendering](#pixel-perfect-rendering); opening a map scene directly (F6) plays it without the hub or the pixel view.

- **Feathers and the shop:** a run earns feathers (the shop's currency; PLACEHOLDER amounts): 1 per kill, 10 per guard, 15 per shrine, 50 for the sin (100 for Satan), and 5 per feather gem that horde enemies sometimes drop (3% chance, scaled by luck; pale-gold PLACEHOLDER gems pulled in like experience), multiplied by the run's difficulty tier (x1 / x1.5 / x2 / x3 for Penance / Purgatory / Damnation / Abyss, rounded down). The HUD counts them. When a hub-launched run ends they are banked: all of them on a win, half (rounded down) on a defeat, and the end card says how many. R waits until the defeat card is up, so retrying never skips banking; very large counters on the HUD shorten to thousands (12.3K). Press **B** or click **SHOP** on the pentagram (which shows the banked total) to spend them on permanent ranks: Vitality (+10 max health), Might (+5% damage), Armor (hits 1 less), Haste (cooldowns 4% shorter), Luck (+10% drops), Swiftness (+10 speed), Pull (+15 pickup radius), and Wings (+0.3 s of flight), five ranks each at rising prices (`Progress.UPGRADES` in `eight/scripts/progress.gd`; names, values, and prices are PLACEHOLDER). Rows show their rank as filled and empty diamonds; a purchase saves at once, and one that cannot be saved is undone and reported. Every realm entered from the pentagram starts with the bought ranks; maps played directly (F6) and the tests do not.
- **Endings:** leaving a realm through its portal (or slaying Satan) wins it: the horde vanishes, the angel rises back into the sky in its beam while the whole realm heals beneath it, the win is saved, and the game returns to the pentagram (R does nothing during the ascension or a hosted win, so the ascension and save always finish; a map played directly with F6 can be restarted with R once the ascension ends). If the save cannot be written, the end card says so and offers **Try saving again** or **Return without saving**; it never returns as if it had saved, and only a successful save is called "saved". If the angel dies, it falls, the world dims, and a card offers **Retry** (or R) and **Return to the pentagram**.
- **Horde mix per sin (PLACEHOLDER):** each realm weights the twelve horde types (`Run.HUSK` ... `Run.MIRROR` in `ENEMY_TYPES`: husk, imp, brute, flyer, charger, ground caster, bomber, splitter, shielder, mortar, sleeper, mirror) differently among the ones its clock has unlocked: Pride balanced with chargers and shielders, Greed imps, ground casters, and mortars, Lust imps, flyers, and ground casters, Envy flyers, splitters, and mirrors, Gluttony brutes and splitters, Wrath imps, brutes, chargers, and bombers, Sloth husks, sleepers, and mortars with 40% slower spawns, Satan all twelve. The tables are `ENEMY_UNLOCK`, `SIN_ENEMY_WEIGHTS`, and `SIN_SPAWN_PACE` in `eight/scripts/run.gd`.
- **Satan's realm:** no shrines and no countdown. It stays calm for a short ritual after landing (3 s, PLACEHOLDER; the HUD counts it down), then Satan arrives with the horde; his fall wins at once, with no portal.

## Survival run

The first map is a heavily corrupted plain. The run opens with the angel, fully robed in purple and riding a black ox, descending from the sky in a beam of light and landing on a scorched sigil; enemies only start spawning after touchdown. Hordes then spawn just off-screen and grow denser over time.

- **Light spear combo:** hold attack to chain thrust, thrust, and a sweeping finisher. The finisher swings the light trident through a 140-degree arc, hitting every enemy inside it for double damage (walls and obstacles still block it). Pausing longer than the combo window restarts the chain. Spear hits land with a brief hit-stop and camera shake (stronger on the finisher) and knock enemies back.
- **Bow combo:** press Q or turn the mouse wheel to switch between spear and bow; switching cancels the combo in progress. The bow follows the spear's rhythm: press or hold attack to chain shot, quicker shot, and finisher, each firing one arrow at the mouse; pausing longer than the combo window restarts the chain. Normal shots deal PLACEHOLDER damage, range, and pierce (tunable in the player's **Bow** export group) and shake the camera lightly on release. The finisher (evolved or not, except when **Rain of Light** replaces the arrow) deals 1.5x damage, gains +1 pierce, and fires a heavier, brighter arrow (PLACEHOLDER); its release shakes slightly harder, and its first hit shakes and briefly slows time. Arrows are unlimited and stop at walls and obstacles. The bow and the arrows are drawn PLACEHOLDER shapes, and the shot reuses the placeholder thrust sound.
- **Weapon evolution:** every shrine guard drops an evolution item when slain, and horde enemies very rarely do (`evolution_drop_chance` on the `Run` node). Each item is for one weapon (PLACEHOLDER item art tinted mint for the spear, violet for the bow) and must be walked over. Collecting it pauses on a choice of that weapon's two paths (click or press 1-2, with a flash); a level-up that arrives meanwhile waits until after. Each path replaces the weapon's finisher for the rest of the run, and each weapon evolves once per run (a spare item gives 10 experience, and drops stop for an evolved weapon). The weapon gauge then shows the path's name. Spear: **Whirlwind** (the finisher spins a full circle, striking all around) or **Chain Light** (lightning jumps from the finisher's first hit to up to 3 more enemies within 200 px, each taking the finisher's damage). Bow: **Ricochet** (the finishing arrow bounces on to up to 4 more enemies within 280 px) or **Rain of Light** (the finishing shot marks a 110 px circle at the aim, within bow range; after 0.35 s light falls, every enemy inside, on the ground or in the air, takes double bow damage, and landing on a target shakes the camera and briefly slows time). Names, wording, numbers, and the lightning, telegraph, and streak visuals are PLACEHOLDER (tunable in the player's **Evolution** export group and `EVOLUTIONS` in `eight/scripts/run.gd`).
- **Side weapons:** four auto-firing side weapons strike ground and flying enemies alike, never through walls or standing obstacles, and rest while the angel descends or has fallen; when a run ends, anything still in flight (arrows, feathers, falling light) is removed and chests stay shut. **Orbiting halos** (2-4 light orbs circling the angel; each target struck at most twice a second), **Seeking feathers** (every 1.6 s, 1-3 gold feathers at the nearest enemies within 420 px; they wait for a target), **Holy pulse** (every 3 s, a ring of 120-180 px that strikes and pushes back), and **Judgment bolt** (light strikes a random enemy within 560 px every 2.4-1.5 s). They come from relics: walk up to one of the realm's chests (three per map, placed from the map's seed on open ground away from the landing site, unless the map scene authors its own under `Chests`; portals and spawns keep clear of them) to open it, or slay a shrine guard; a gold relic spills out (PLACEHOLDER item art). Collecting a relic finds a random side weapon the angel lacks or levels one it has (up to level III, more orbs, feathers, damage, reach, or speed); once all four are at III, a relic gives 10 experience. The HUD lists them at the bottom left and announces each find. Every number and the orbs, ring, bolt, and chest are PLACEHOLDER (constants at the top of `eight/scripts/side_weapons.gd`).
- **Hits:** push follows the blow's direction — arrows along their flight, Rain of Light outward from its circle, chain lightning from the previous link, and other hits away from the angel. Horde types have PLACEHOLDER weights (`knockback` in `ENEMY_TYPES`: imps fly furthest, brutes barely move), while bosses and shrine guards cannot be pushed and instead shudder a pixel while flashing. Every connecting hit throws PLACEHOLDER pixel sparks along the push (or away from the angel when unpushable), more and gold for finishers and evolved strikes.
- **Stats from level-ups:** besides the spear, bow, speed, health, pull, and dash upgrades, level-ups offer **Might** (+10% damage from every weapon, side weapons and evolutions included), **Armor** (enemy hits deal 1 less, never below 1; lava burns are not hits), **Haste** (every attack and side-weapon cooldown 8% shorter, down to a floor of 40%; at the floor it is no longer offered), and **Luck** (+20% heart, item, and evolution drop chances). The base values are in the player's **Stats** export group; amounts are PLACEHOLDER.
- **Input buffer:** a dash, take-off, or attack pressed up to 0.15 s before it is ready still fires when it becomes available.
- **Flight:** hold Space to take off. The angel climbs to a hover about 80 px above its ground shadow in 0.2 s, bobs gently while Space is held, and glides back down in 0.25 s when it is released; landing kicks up dust. The climb always finishes, so a tap of Space is a short hop. Flight stamina (2.5 s when full) drains whenever the angel is off the ground and refills only after landing (0.6 s of flight per second, so about 4 s from empty). Taking off needs at least 0.5 s of stamina, so tapping cannot dodge the meter; running dry forces a landing even with Space held, and taking off again needs a fresh press. Pressing Space while gliding down climbs again if stamina allows. Walls and obstacles still block flight; enemies never block the angel's movement, in the air or on the ground. All of this is tunable in the player's **Flight** export group.
- **Air and ground:** while airborne, ground attacks miss the angel: enemy and boss contact and boss projectiles. Attacks from the air (`take_damage(amount, true)`) land at any height. The spear (thrusts and finisher) hits flying enemies only while airborne, and ground enemies and practice targets only on the ground. Arrows hit both from any height, and fly at the height of the bow.
- **Dash:** a fast burst with brief invulnerability that leaves fading PLACEHOLDER afterimages. It works the same in the air: the angel bursts along at its current height while flight and stamina drain continue, and take-off is allowed mid-dash. **Perfect dodge:** a hit that would have landed (not one the angel's height or hurt blink already avoids) within the first 0.1 s of a dash misses, makes the dash ready again as soon as the dash ends, and makes attacks and side weapons 30% quicker for 2 s (PLACEHOLDER timings; one per buff, so dashing through a crowd cannot chain them), with a gold `PERFECT` callout above the angel and a PLACEHOLDER sound.
- **Flying enemies:** after about a minute some horde spawns are flying enemies (PLACEHOLDER imp art, tinted violet). They hover above the plain with a ground shadow and a gentle bob, keep a preferred distance instead of piling on, and fire an aimed shot after a short wind-up glint. Their shots hurt a grounded or airborne angel and pass over walls and pillars, and touching one hurts from the air. The bow hits them anywhere; the spear reaches them only while the angel is airborne. They do not collide with walls, pillars, or each other.
- **Chargers, ground casters, bombers, splitters, shielders, mortars, sleepers, and mirrors (PLACEHOLDER):** eight more horde enemies unlock over the run (`Run.CHARGER` ... `Run.MIRROR`), reusing existing placeholder sprites tinted in their scenes (`eight/scenes/charger.tscn`, `ground_caster.tscn`, `bomber.tscn`, `splitter.tscn`, `shielder.tscn`, `mortar.tscn`, `sleeper.tscn`, `mirror.tscn`). **Chargers** (`eight/scripts/charger.gd`, red-tinted brute art; Pride and Wrath, 1:30) chase until within 260 px, stop to show an ember lane clipped at walls and obstacles for 0.5 s, dash for 0.4 s (blows cannot shove them off the lane they showed), rest for 0.6 s as a punish window, then wait 1 s before the next lane, so a pack staggers its charges. **Ground casters** (`eight/scripts/ground_caster.gd`, gold-tinted imp art; Greed and Lust, 1:15) keep about 220 px like the flyer but on the ground, show a short ember glint (their aim locks when it starts, as flyers' now does too, so stepping aside dodges the shot; casters spawned together do not fire in lockstep), and fire ground shots that walls and obstacles stop and an airborne angel flies over. **Bombers** (`eight/scripts/bomber.gd`, orange-tinted imp art; Wrath, 2:30) run in without hurting on contact, light a 0.7 s fuse with an ember circle within 60 px (a lit bomber is hard to shove), and detonate a ground blast (72 px) that hurts the angel and every ground enemy inside (not flyers), its damage to enemies scaling with tier and time like horde health; the bomber dies in the blast and counts as a kill, and if slain before the fuse finishes it does not explode. **Splitters** (`eight/scripts/run.gd`, green-tinted brute art; Envy and Gluttony, 2:00) die into two husks placed either side of the line to the angel (never on top of it) that never split again, within the horde cap (`max_enemies`). **Shielders** (`eight/scripts/shielder.gd`, steel-tinted brute art with a code-drawn shield arc; Pride, 2:15) turn slowly (75 degrees per second) toward the angel and block blows from inside their 120-degree front cone (`GUARD_HALF_ANGLE = PI / 3.0`) with a shield flash, sparks, and a PLACEHOLDER thud but no damage and no push; a blocked thrust or arrow stops at the shield instead of piercing on; finishers and evolved strikes (heavy hits) break the guard for 1.5 s and land, while blows from the side or behind always land. **Mortars** (`eight/scripts/mortar.gd` and `ground_strike.gd`, brown-tinted brute art; Greed and Sloth, 2:00) keep far back (`preferred_distance = 300` px), hold their fire while the angel is off screen, and, every 3.8 s after a 0.5 s wind-up (no glint beforehand), mark a 56 px ember circle under the angel that lands 0.8 s later as a ground attack (flying over or dashing through avoids it). **Sleepers** (`eight/scripts/sleeper.gd`, grey-blue-tinted husk art; Sloth, 1:45) lie still until the angel comes within 150 px or strikes them, wake behind a 0.4 s ring, then move 1.45x as fast and pulse a 110 px ring every 3.8 s (0.75 s fill tell) that slows a grounded angel's walking to 68% speed for 1.2 s (dashes and flight are unaffected, and flying avoids the pulse; overlapping slows each keep their own time: the strongest applies, and a weaker, longer one takes over when it ends), tinting the angel blue-grey and showing `SLOWED` on the HUD (alongside this `apply_slow` status, a telegraphed `apply_pull` adds to the angel's walking for a moment; dashes ignore it). **Mirrors** (`eight/scripts/mirror.gd`, mint-tinted imp art; Envy, 3:00) copy the angel's weapon: they rush all the way in and start no new shot against the spear (a shot already winding up still fires where its glint showed), and keep 240 px away firing ground shots (ember glint, locked aim) against the bow. Satan's realm includes all twelve horde types once unlocked. Once the angel falls, a lit fuse stops, marked mortar circles vanish, and nothing killed after the run ends is counted or pays; the ascent clears a slow.
- **Lava pools:** every sin map scatters a handful of irregular molten pools on the floor, drawn under actors and pickups with bright hot colors (PLACEHOLDER colors: orange molten rock with over-bright bubbles that bloom under the HDR glow) and a slow shimmer. The layout is deterministic per sin map, kept clear of the landing site, walls, and obstacles, and spaced apart. Standing in one burns 8 health every 0.5 s after a 0.4 s grace (PLACEHOLDER balance, tunable on the Lava node), with an orange flash, the hurt sound, and a BURNING callout as feedback. Flying over lava and dashing through it are safe and reset the burn timer, and the hurt-blink from an enemy hit delays a burn tick instead of being ignored — burn ticks never start or extend the invulnerability window, so lava cannot shield from enemies. Only the angel burns: ground enemies, bosses, and projectiles are unharmed and unslowed, and lava never blocks movement. `run.point_in_lava(point, inset)` answers "is this point in lava?" for placement, e.g. keeping the exit portal clear.

- **Breakable obstacles:** every sin map has wall segments, structures (where the old pillars stood), and trees, each with PLACEHOLDER toughness: a tree takes 3 damage, a wall segment 6, a structure 10. Spear thrusts deal 1, the finisher 2, arrows 1 (plus upgrades and items), so a tree falls to three thrusts or three arrows and a structure to ten thrusts. A weapon that hits an obstacle spends itself on it: a thrust or arrow stops there whatever its pierce, and the finisher strikes enemies in its arc only where no obstacle stands between, so nothing behind an obstacle is hit until it breaks (the blow that breaks it included). Spear blows on obstacles give the same hit-stop and shake as on enemies. A blow flashes and shakes the obstacle and plays the enemy hit sound; glowing cracks and darkening show three damage stages, and a dark hairline marks every obstacle as breakable before the first blow. At zero it bursts into debris with a poof and a thud (the placeholder landing sound), and it stops blocking at once. While standing, obstacles block like walls: the angel on the ground and in flight, ground enemies and bosses, and ground shots. Flying enemies and their shots pass over them, and nothing but the player's weapons damages them. The arena's boundary walls cannot be broken. Each sin map builds its own layout from a fixed seed (Satan's 5 shrines, 6 structures, 8 wall segments, 40 trees) and keeps the landing site clear and 140 px lanes between obstacles and from the arena walls. Portals, horde spawns, bosses, and summoned minions are placed clear of standing obstacles. Kinds and toughness live in `eight/scripts/breakable.gd`; counts and lanes at the top of `eight/scripts/run.gd`. The obstacle visuals are PLACEHOLDER: structures reuse the pillar art, walls and trees are drawn shapes.
- **Life:** 100 health to start, shown as a bottom-filled orb. Enemy and boss contact costs 8-25 health depending on the enemy, and boss projectiles cost 12 at the start on Penance (more on harder tiers and as the clock runs); taking a hit flashes the angel red, shakes the camera, briefly slows time, flashes the screen edge crimson (PLACEHOLDER), and grants a short invulnerability blink. At 25% life or less, the screen edge pulses crimson. Dashing also grants invulnerability and passes through projectiles. Boss projectiles stop at walls and obstacles, and check their entire movement each physics tick so fast shots cannot skip through the player. Flying-enemy shots cost 10 and reach an airborne angel, while ground enemies and boss contact and shots miss one. Running out of health ends the run.
- **Experience:** slain enemies drop experience gems, which are pulled in when you come near. Filling the bar at the top raises your level, pauses the game, and offers three upgrades; pick one by clicking or pressing 1-3.
- **Life pickups:** enemies sometimes drop life, and each boss drops two. Life stays on the ground while you are at full health.
- **Items:** enemies rarely drop an item, and the slain sin drops one. Walk over it to collect it. The top-right list appears after collecting an item and shows the latest six, with a total count. Every collected bonus remains active for the run.
- **Satan's shrines and the sin:** each run is one sin's map (`sin_index` on the `Run` node; the default is Pride). On every sin's map (not Satan's own), five of Satan's shrines stand on the points of a pentagram 1300 px around the landing site, each a red-glowing obelisk with a weaker copy of the sin beside it as its guard (35% health; it defends while the angel is within 520 px of its shrine and walks back otherwise). An **8-minute countdown** starts on landing: if any shrine still stands when it runs out, the angel dies. A shrine takes 20 blows from the player's weapons (only the player can damage it) and can be hit while its guard lives; breaking one heals the ground around it (a PLACEHOLDER green circle with a soft light). Breaking the last shrine stops the countdown and summons the sin at full strength. Slaying the sin wins the map: a portal opens near its death site, clear of walls and obstacles, and the run keeps going, with the horde still scaling and the angel still leveling, until you leave. Stand inside the portal, then press and hold E for one second to leave; releasing or stepping out cancels, and holding E before arriving does not activate it. Gold diamonds on a ring around the angel point toward shrines that are off screen, and once the sin falls a larger mint diamond points to the exit while it is off screen. Counts, distances, toughness, guard strength and the countdown are PLACEHOLDER values at the top of `eight/scripts/run.gd` and on the `Run` node.
- **Boss and guard attacks:** bosses and shrine guards only start attacks while the angel is on screen (alive and neither descending nor ascending). Every attack winds up with a stepped telegraph in the locked aim direction (ember lines on a dark backing for ground attacks — charges, bursts, aimed fans, coin rings, slams, and shockwaves, which you can fly over or dash through — and pale marks for summons; guards never summon), lands, then leaves the boss standing still in a recovery it keeps even if the angel leaves a guard's leash (0.8 s after a charge, which ends far away, 0.5 s otherwise; 0.6 s after a slam, shockwave, or rain). Bosses can chain attacks into combos: each step shows its own tell before it lands, and the boss rests only after the last step. A charge lane is drawn only as far as the boss can travel before a wall or obstacle stops it, and a summon's marks are the exact open-ground spots its minions will appear on. Boss shots scale with the tier and the clock like contact damage (12 on Penance at the start). Crossing a health threshold (half health by default) enters a faster phase (0.75x attack interval per phase): the boss poses for 0.8 s (standing still while still hittable, calling off any attack in progress), and a sin can bring its own patterns, combos, and speed multipliers per phase. **Pride** (the first redesigned sin, 100 base health): phase one is the aimed fan and the charge; at half health it adds a guard stance (a 0.35 s tell, then 1.5 s standing behind a shield arc that turns slowly and blocks blows from its front, like a shielder — spear thrusts and arrows stop at any raised guard, shielder or boss; finishers and evolved strikes break it from any side, landing and leaving Pride open for 1.2 s with no slam) that chains into a slam through the combo system: a 110 px ember circle around Pride that lands after 0.7 s (fly over it or step out). Its shrine guards fight with phase one only, with no combos. **Wrath** (the second redesigned sin, 200 base health): phase one uses its aimed fan alongside a double-charge combo (the second charge heads for where the angel stood when the combo began) that ends in a quick 90 px slam (0.5 s tell) where it stops. At half health it enrages: 20% faster and attacking more often, with a burst ring after each charge (charge, burst, charge, burst, slam). At a fifth of its health it adds a shockwave (0.6 s tell): three 12 px ember rings rolling out 0.45 s apart to 420 px (far enough apart for a dash or a step between them), each hurting a grounded angel once (fly over them or dash through); they are drawn like the other tells, a dim band between crisp ember edges, and what is drawn is what hits. Phase three keeps the enrage's pace rather than speeding Wrath up again (`phase_paces`). Its shrine guards fight with phase one only, with no combos. **Greed** (the third redesigned sin, 130 base health): phase one throws coin rings (12 directions with a gap right beside the shot aimed at the angel on each side: step into a gap at any range, or fly over) and summons ground casters. At 60% health it adds rain: five 48 px violet air circles around the angel (one on it, four 100 px out) landing one after another from 0.8 s, 0.15 s apart; flying does not help, move out or dash. At a quarter health its coin volleys spiral (three rings that keep the volley's first aim, each turned half a slot, so a step with the spiral stays safe). Its shrine guards only throw coins on Penance (harder tiers add the tier's generic attacks). **Lust** (the fourth redesigned sin, 110 base health, the fastest sin): phase one charges and blows kisses (a 0.6 s violet tell, then three violet air shots at 185 px/s in a tight fan: flying does not help, dash through or step aside; Lust then rests 0.6 s). At half health it adds a blink: a pale ring draws in around Lust with a tick pointing where it will go, and a pale circle marks a spot 120 px behind the angel (as seen from Lust; in front on open ground inside the arena if that is blocked by a wall, an obstacle, the angel or a ground enemy). Lust appears there after 0.6 s and at once charges at the angel behind a quicker 0.5 s lane; if the angel has walked onto the marked spot by then, the blink is called off instead. Its shrine guards fight with phase one only. **Gluttony** (the fifth redesigned sin, 220 base health): phase one leaps and vomits: a 1.0 s 120 px ember circle marks where the angel stands, Gluttony lands there (a ground hit, its flash covering that circle) and leaves a 90 px bile puddle for 4 s that hurts a grounded angel standing in it (it tries every 0.5 s; the hurt blink spaces the hits to about once a second; fly over it or stay out); the vomit is a 9-shot ground fan. At half health it adds a devour: after a 0.5 s violet tell, a 1.2 s inhale cone (260 px) pulls the angel (slower than walking; a dash ignores it) and the horde toward its mouth; horde enemies reaching the mouth are eaten (they are simply gone: no kill, no loot, and Gluttony never heals); when it closes, an angel still at the mouth is bitten (1.5x contact damage), even in the air. At a fifth of its health it summons splitters. Its shrine guards leap and vomit on Penance (harder tiers add the tier's generic attacks). Smarter tiers (Damnation and Abyss) lead their aimed fans partway (60%) toward where the angel is heading (charges never lead). Smarter **Pride** adds tier behaviors: from Purgatory each charge chains once into a second lane, re-aimed at the angel, via the combo system; from Damnation about half of its aimed fans come from the air (violet tells with an arc across the fan's mouth, a violet glow, and violet-glowing shots that reach a flying angel; dash or step out). Smarter **Wrath**: from Purgatory its second charge re-aims at the angel; from Damnation it summons bombers only once enraged (a sin with its own summon rule does not get the tier's generic summon). Smarter **Greed**: from Purgatory each volley gains a coin wave; from Damnation its rain lands where the angel is heading (0.35 s of walking ahead; a dash does not throw it further). Smarter **Lust**: from Purgatory it blinks and charges twice in a row; from Damnation it summons flyers once past half health. ## Pixel-perfect rendering

Every art pixel on screen is the same size. `eight/scenes/game.tscn` (`eight/scripts/game.gd`) renders the run into a fixed **640x360** SubViewport (one art pixel = 2 world units = 1 buffer pixel; the camera zoom is 0.5, so gameplay units, speeds, and ranges are unchanged) and shows it at the largest **whole-number** scale that fits the window, centered, with dark bars in any leftover space: 1280x720 is 2x, 1920x1080 is 3x, 2560x1440 is 4x, 3840x2160 is 6x, and in-between or non-16:9 windows (1366x768, ultrawide) keep the nearest smaller scale with bars. The minimum window is 1280x720. Sprites, rotated weapons, drawn shapes, lights, and glow all rasterize into that one buffer with nearest sampling. The HUD is moved out of the buffer to the native-resolution root, so UI text stays crisp; it can sit over the bars.

Every sprite draws at exactly 2x. ## UI

The UI is crisp vector UI drawn by Godot from one shared theme, `eight/resources/ui_theme.tres`: the repo palette (void black, plum frames, pale mint text, pale gold highlights), **Alegreya Sans** for body and HUD text, **Marcellus** for headings and buttons (both SIL OFL 1.1; see `eight/assets/fonts/CREDITS.md`), and chamfered frames whose hover and panel states glow through StyleBox shadows. Type variations in the theme: `HeadingLabel` (Marcellus, gold), `MenuPanel` (glowing chamfered panel), and `ChoiceButton` (level-up cards). New screens should use the theme rather than inline styles.

- **Scaling:** the UI scales with the window at half the world's whole-number scale (1x at 1280x720, 1.5x at 1920x1080, 2x at 2560x1440) through the root content scale, so it keeps its size relative to the game and the text is re-rendered sharp at each size.
- **Pause:** Esc opens a pause menu (Resume, Restart run) and pauses the world; Esc or Resume closes it. It never opens over a level-up choice or after the run ends, closing it never unpauses a pending level-up, and a level-up that arrives while it is open replaces it.
- **Motion:** level-up panels and the pause menu pop in with a short overshoot, and the three level-up cards deal in one after another. UI tweens run while the tree is paused and ignore hit-stop slow-motion.
- **World tags:** the boss name and the portal's `EXIT [HOLD E]` prompt are HUD labels that follow their world position (`Hud.world_to_hud`), so they stay crisp instead of being drawn into the pixel buffer. A tag hides while its anchor is outside the world view, so it never floats in the bars.

## Settings

**SETTINGS** on the pentagram and in the pause menu opens a panel that saves to `user://settings.cfg` (separate from progress) and applies at once, mid-run included:

- **Quality** (slider): each step adds to the one below. **Low**: no glow, no lamp shadows, no extra effects. **Medium**: glow, hard lamp shadows, lava shimmer, pulsing sin eyes. **High** (default): adds soft shadows, world haze, and a wider bloom. **Ultra**: adds two-tone light pools.
- **VSync**, a **frame cap** (30 / 60 / 120 / unlimited), and **fullscreen**.
- **DAMAGE NUMBERS** (on/off), **SCREEN SHAKE** (0% / 50% / 100%), and **HIT-STOP** (on/off).

Settings apply to hub-launched realms and the window; a map opened directly (F6) and the tests keep the scene defaults, which match High with all feel options on. On the development PC (1920x1080, 60 enemies on screen, VSync off) every quality step measured about 1.7-1.8 ms per frame, so the slider matters most on weaker GPUs; VSync and the frame cap are the bigger levers there. `settings_smoke.gd` (with its own files) checks the defaults, damaged settings falling back, a round trip, the frame cap, the effects each step turns on, and the panel from the pause menu (live changes, saved at once, Esc back to the pause menu) and from the pentagram (keys held while open, a click outside the panel caught by its dim layer, Esc closes), with R and F3 held mid-run while it is open. Effects a quality step switches off skip their shader work entirely, and every resource the setting changes belongs to its own run.

## Shaders, glow and lighting

All look-development shaders, materials and light values are **PLACEHOLDER-tunable**, pending human art direction. No new art or audio is included. Forward Plus/D3D12 is preserved.

The run is dark: away from light the world shows as dim silhouettes, and glowing things are real light sources that reveal what is around them. There are three layers, all on canvas layer 0 so the result still blooms; Atmosphere is layer 1 and the HUD is layer 2.

Phase 8 look-development (all PLACEHOLDER-tunable, layered by the Quality setting): **lava shimmer** (`eight/shaders/lava.gdshader`: bright and dark ripples crawl across molten lava per art pixel, in stepped time), **world haze** (`atmosphere.gdshader`: purple fog patches pinned to the world, sampled per art pixel, stepped in time and dithered into four levels; `Run` feeds it the camera's world area each frame), **soft shadows** (the angel's Lamp uses a PCF13 shadow filter whose soft edge the light bands dither onto the grid), a **wider bloom** (glow level 3), a stepped **eye pulse** on the sins' glow (`emissive.gdshader` `pulse`), and **two-tone light** (`light.gdshader` `core_whiten`: whiter cores, coloured skirts; Ultra only).

1. **Glow.** HDR 2D and the run's Canvas-mode WorldEnvironment let colours above 1.0 (including drawn lava) bloom. Tight low glow levels and linear upscaling keep pixel edges clear. Tune `glow_intensity`, `glow_hdr_threshold` and `glow_levels` on WorldEnvironment.
2. **What glows: the light palette** (`eight/shaders/emissive.gdshader`). A sprite pixel painted in the material's `key` colour glows as `glow` x `energy`; with `whole` on, every pixel glows (things made of light). The key is matched on the original texture, so hit flashes and tints cannot switch a glow off. The materials beside the shader are the palette:
   - `eyes.tres`: **cream `#e6e29e` glows red** on every enemy and boss.
   - `player_light.tres`: **mint `#caebe0` glows blue-white** on the angel, and its cream halo glows gold.
   - `weapon_light.tres` (spear, bow, arrows, exit portal): cool whole-sprite glow; `enemy_ammo.tres`: hot orange-gold whole-sprite glow so enemy shots stay distinct from red enemies.
   - `pickup_light.tres`: every XP, life and item pickup glows in its own colours.
3. **What gets lit: darkness and lights** (`Lighting`, a CanvasGroup above the world in `run.tscn`). Its children draw light; `eight/shaders/lighting.gdshader` then multiplies the finished world by `ambient` + that light. Light is read once per art pixel (2 px) and stepped into dithered bands, so it stays on the pixel-art grid and does not swim when the camera moves. The camera snaps its final translation to whole world-buffer pixels; this intentionally trades away sub-pixel scrolling to keep the light cells stable. `Murk` is a faint additive haze drawn just below, so light pools read on the dark floor.

**Art rule for hand-made sprites:** to make something glow, paint it in the reserved colour: mint on the angel, cream on enemies. Nothing is set up per sprite. The cost is that those exact colours cannot be used for non-glowing detail on those sprites; on the placeholders, horn tips and teeth are cream, so they glow with the eyes. Greed and Sloth have dark pupils, so their cream details—not their pupils—glow until their placeholder art is repainted.

**Adding a light** takes one line: `add_to_group(&"light")` on a CanvasItem (or `groups=["light"]` in its scene). `eight/scripts/lights.gd` draws one falloff quad per visible member each frame, and a freed node leaves the group by itself. A light is two metadata values, `light` (a Color whose alpha is energy / 4) and `light_radius` (px), read from the node or, when the node has none, from its material. So a family is tuned once in its material (`eyes.tres`, `enemy_ammo.tres`, `weapon_light.tres`), and one kind can override it on its node (the boss sprite in `boss.tscn`, the exit portal in `exit_portal.tscn`). Lava makes one orange light per blob (`LIGHT_COLOR`, `LIGHT_REACH` in `lava_pools.gd`). Current lights: the angel's wings and halo, spear, bow and descent beam, enemy and boss sprites, enemy shots, arrows, pickups, the exit portal, and lava. Hit effects are not lights.

**Only the angel's light casts shadows.** `Lamp` (a PointLight2D on the player) lights nothing but `Lighting/AngelLight`, a light-only additive sheet, so its shadowed light joins the same sum as every other light. The lamp follows the angel's height and fades while descending, so the landing site stays dim until touchdown. Each `Breakable` carries a `LightOccluder2D` shaped like the drawn obstacle (a tree's trunk or the actual pillar art inside its texture), culled so the obstacle itself stays lit and its shadow falls behind it; breaking the obstacle removes the shadow. The Lamp's texture must fade by alpha, because a light-only item shows the light texture's alpha, and `shadow_item_cull_mask` must include AngelLight's light mask (2) as well as the occluders' (1), or no shadow is received.
